## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**Not slop.** Verified against DESIGN.md's own banned list directly: no gradient text, no glassmorphism-by-default, no generic hero-metric-card (the live-temp badge is load-bearing, sets up the LST-vs-air-temp disclaimer). Semantic data colors match the token table everywhere, both themes. One soft violation: an identical 2x2 bordered-icon-card grid appears on both /mission and /contribute.

**Deterministic scan:** 0 findings (clean run, both before and after this session's changes).

## Overall Impression

The product's actual substance holds up under real interaction, not just on the happy path (verified two wards side by side, genuinely different reasoning). The honesty/limitations framing is unusually well-executed for this category. The two real gaps are architectural, not cosmetic: no browser history/URL state on ward selection (Back button ejects you), and the search box doesn't speak the vocabulary of the site's own stated #1 user (residents searching their neighborhood, not a ward code).

## What's Working

1. The intervention engine is real, not templated — different wards get genuinely different factor breakdowns, intervention counts, and citations.
2. Honesty is executed, not just stated — proxy/limitation caveats sit next to the specific number they qualify, not quarantined to one page.
3. Accessibility foundations were actually built, not skipped — real ARIA tabs with roving tabindex, full keyboard-only path to every ward via the sidebar list.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] Browser Back exits the dashboard entirely**
Why it matters: the most natural "undo" gesture discards all state and ejects to an unrelated page, right after the trust-building moment of viewing a ward's breakdown.
Fix: push a history entry or `?ward=` query param on selection so Back closes the detail view first.
Suggested command: /impeccable polish

**[P1] Search doesn't match the site's own stated #1 user's vocabulary**
Why it matters: "Andheri" and "Malabar Hill" (both real, both listed under their ward) return "No ward matches." Search only matches ward codes. Directly undercuts PRODUCT.md's "curious residents, no GIS background" persona.
Fix: index the areas/localities list into the search matcher.
Suggested command: /impeccable clarify

**[P2] Default map framing wastes ~80% of the screen**
Why it matters: ward polygons span only ~20% of the map container width at first load; most of the view is Thane/Panvel/ocean.
Fix: fitBounds() to the ward layer's extent instead of a fixed center/zoom.
Suggested command: /impeccable layout

**[P2] Leaflet map container has no accessible name**
Why it matters: a screen reader tabbing through announces zoom-button/attribution text with no indication it's a heat-vulnerability map.
Fix: add aria-label="Mumbai ward heat vulnerability map" to the container.
Suggested command: /impeccable harden

**[P3] Dead, duplicate /support page**
Why it matters: unlinked from every nav and footer, reproduces ~80% of /contribute's content. Content debt on a site whose pitch is "built in the open."
Fix: delete it or fold into /contribute.
Suggested command: /impeccable distill

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (confused first-timer, general public):** hits both P1s first — searches their neighborhood, gets nothing; finds their ward, hits Back to check another, gets bounced to the landing page.

**Sam (accessibility):** layer switcher is a model ARIA-tabs implementation. Map container itself is a broken tab stop, but a full keyboard-only path exists via the sidebar list. At 900-960px width nothing overlapped or clipped.

**Riley (stress tester):** the Back-button trap is the standout finding. Refresh mid-flow is harmless by comparison — dashboard always resets cleanly.

## Questions to Consider

1. PRODUCT.md names "curious residents, no GIS background" as user #1, but the dashboard's actual vocabulary is BMC ward-code shorthand — was the dashboard designed for user #1, or for #2/#3 with a landing page bolted on to make #1 feel invited?
2. Every intervention carries a citation, none carry a cost or feasibility note — is "decision-support" the right claim when the tool stops exactly where a real planner's decision would get made?
3. The credibility pitch leans on "built in the open," but the repo isn't public yet — how much of that trust argument rests on a promise rather than a link a visitor can click today?
